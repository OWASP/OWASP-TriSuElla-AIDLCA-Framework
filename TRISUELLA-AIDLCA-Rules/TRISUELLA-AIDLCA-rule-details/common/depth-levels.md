# TRISU Adaptive Depth & Strategic Precision

## Core Objective
The **TRISU** framework is designed for **Strategic Precision**. It adapts the level of detail and rigor (Depth) to the specific complexity and risk profile of the project. 

While every stage in an active phase generates its mandatory TRISU artifacts, the density of information within those artifacts is dynamic.

---

## Technical Multipliers of Depth

The system evaluates several vectors to determine the required depth of governance:

1. **Strategic Risk**: High-stakes environments (e.g., BFSI, Healthcare) demand **Comprehensive** depth to satisfy **TRISU-ISO-IND** or **TRISU-DATA** invariants.
2. **Structural Complexity**: Multi-agent orchestrations and distributed microservices require detailed interface definitions and **TRISU-TEST** property proofs.
3. **Intent Clarity**: Vague steward intent triggers the **DUGNAD** clarification protocol, increasing the depth of requirements discovery.
4. **Architectural Impact**: System-wide migrations or core logic refactors require maximum depth to ensure **SISU** (Resilience).

---

## Depth Levels in Practice

### 🍃 Minimal Precision (Low Risk / Routine)
- **Focus**: Efficiency and speed without compromising base invariants.
- **Artifacts**: Concise, focused on immediate logical changes. 
- **Example**: A simple bug fix in a non-critical utility.

### 🏛️ Standard Precision (Default / Complex)
- **Focus**: Balanced governance, complete documentation, and standard security gates.
- **Artifacts**: Detailed functional descriptions, standard threat models, and integration tests.
- **Example**: Development of a new feature set within an existing service.

### 🛡️ Comprehensive Precision (High Risk / Sovereign)
- **Focus**: Zero-Trust enforcement, absolute traceability, and formal verification.
- **Artifacts**: Multi-round DUGNAD questioning, detailed property-based invariants, and exhaustive jurisdictional compliance mapping.
- **Example**: Deployment of an autonomous agent in a regulated financial environment.

---

## The TRISU Efficiency Principle

> **"Apply exactly the governance required by the risk, and no more."**

- Simple logic should not be burdened by excessive documentation.
- Complex logic MUST NOT be shortchanged by abbreviated analysis.
- The **TILLIT** (Trust) pillar remains the final arbiter: if the risk is high, the depth MUST be comprehensive.
